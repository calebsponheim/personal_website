# About

Hi, I'm Caleb.

I research emerging AI, build things to understand it better, and teach people how to use and design it responsibly.

I'm a Senior Experience Specialist and the AI Lead at [NN/G](https://www.nngroup.com/people/caleb-sponheim/). A lot of my work is at the intersection of AI and User Experience. I try and figure out whether people can rely on AI in their own work, or users can rely on it in a product experience.

I enjoy staying current with AI, learning about new capabilities, and applying them to learn how they work. It's then satisfying to take those insights and figure out how to present and teach them.

## AI Work

I developed an AI evaluation framework called [PROVE](/projects/prove/) to help people evaluate an AI tool against a specific task. It asks learners to consider the quality of the output, the time spent checking and correcting it, and the friction of using the tool repeatedly. Getting an answer quickly doesn't help much if fixing it takes longer than doing the work yourself.

I also created [Designing AI Experiences](https://www.nngroup.com/courses/designing-ai-experiences/), a full-day course through NN/G, as well as designed and built [Lemon](/projects/lemon/), NN/G's institutional AI agent. Building Lemon puts our advice to work: I have to decide what it can access, how it explains itself, and when a colleague needs to approve an action. Those decisions give me material to investigate and bring back into my writing and teaching.

My [research](/projects/neuroscience-research/) involved brain–machine interfaces, neural recordings, and how brain activity relates to movement. I spent a lot of time working with uncertain evidence and explaining findings to people with very different expertise.

I'm also a theater kid and an IT nerd. ## What matters to me

I use AI every day, and I care about what adopting it asks of people. Someone using a system should be able to understand its limits, question an answer, and retain control over consequential decisions. Sometimes the useful recommendation is to keep doing the task without AI.

I also care about the infrastructure behind these tools. Data centers should run on clean energy, and the people who live around them should have a say in what gets built. Those consequences are part of deciding whether and how to use a technology.
