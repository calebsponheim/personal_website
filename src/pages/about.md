# About

I'm a former neuroscientist and I carry the guilt and trauma of grad school with me. I'm an AI expert who thinks that AI models are impactful and powerful, and also that tech companies have made some of the most destructive decisions in human history.

I use AI every day to accelerate my work and do more than I could before, while also strongly believing that data centers should never be powered by fossil fuels and should stay out of residential neighborhoods. Yes, that's a contradiction. I hold it anyway.

Growing up, I learned that I could emotionally express myself on the stage, but not anywhere else. Now, I've taught thousands of people how to design and use technology responsibly and respectfully using those same skills.

I have a bundle of skills that pull together to represent... a person with an anxiety disorder and a love of technology. I've rigged lighting, built interactive art, worked helpdesk, assembled many video kits, and generally been the person in the room who volunteers to figure out the thing nobody else wants to touch.

On paper, that adds up to a PhD in computational neuroscience at the University of Chicago, and now a job as Senior Experience Specialist and AI Lead at [NN/G](https://www.nngroup.com/people/caleb-sponheim/), where I turn empirical research into decisions people actually act on. I've been cited as an expert on AI in [Time](https://time.com/7346052/problem-ai-flattering-us/), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/07/07/ai-sycophancy-chatbots-mental-health), [Futurism](https://futurism.com/sycophancy-chatbots-ai-problem), and [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/tech/services-and-software/apples-genmoji-might-be-a-prompt-engineering-training-tool-in-disguise/) — mostly for saying, on the record, that a chatbot flattering you all day might not be doing you any favors.
