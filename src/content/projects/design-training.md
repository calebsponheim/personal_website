At NN/G, I develop and teach live and self-paced courses about AI, UX research, and decision-making. I need to help people understand the technology well enough to make decisions about their own work, even as the tools change around them.

## Designing AI Experiences

I created [Designing AI Experiences](https://www.nngroup.com/courses/designing-ai-experiences/) to help practitioners work through the decisions that come with putting AI in an interface. What does the system let someone do? What will that person expect from it? How should the interface respond when the model can't reliably meet that expectation?

Those questions require some understanding of the technology. A model's capabilities won't necessarily match a person's expectations, so we work through examples of what AI can do and how that affects interaction design. Activities give participants a chance to apply the ideas and explain their choices.

## Bringing research into the classroom

My teaching also covers statistics, quantitative research, and using AI in research workflows. I've developed AI course material, contributed to self-paced courses, and revised examples and activities as the tools and questions have changed.

[PROVE](/projects/prove/) gives people a structure for evaluating a tool against their own work. Building [Lemon](/projects/lemon/) gives me practical experience with the permissions, explanations, and approvals an AI system needs. Both help me connect the advice I teach to decisions I've had to think through myself.

I want someone to leave a course better able to investigate a tool, question an output, or explain a design decision. Examples and activities matter because participants need a chance to do that thinking themselves.
